## Abstract

This historical review examines the contributions of James Lind to naval medicine in the 18th century, with particular focus on his work at Haslar Hospital. We explore his pioneering clinical trial on scurvy and its impact on maritime health.

## Introduction

The 18th century saw significant advances in naval medicine, largely driven by the practical challenges faced by the British Royal Navy. James Lind, appointed as physician to Haslar Hospital in 1758, played a pivotal role in these developments through his empirical approach to medical problems.

## The Scurvy Trial

In 1747, while serving as a ship's surgeon, Lind conducted what is widely regarded as the first controlled clinical trial. We analyze the methodology and findings of this landmark study, which demonstrated the efficacy of citrus fruits in preventing and treating scurvy.

## Haslar Hospital

Established in 1753, Haslar Hospital in Gosport was the largest hospital in England and the first purpose-built naval hospital. We examine Lind's tenure as physician at Haslar and his contributions to hospital administration and patient care.

## Legacy and Influence

Lind's work represents an important transition toward evidence-based medicine. Although the full implementation of his findings on scurvy prevention was delayed, his methodological approach set an important precedent for medical research.

## Conclusion

James Lind's contributions to naval medicine extend beyond his famous scurvy trial. His work at Haslar Hospital helped establish standards for naval healthcare that would influence military medicine for generations to come.
